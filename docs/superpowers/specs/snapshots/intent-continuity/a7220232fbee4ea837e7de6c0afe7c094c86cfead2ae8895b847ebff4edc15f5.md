# Intent continuity — product brief

> **Feature ID:** intent-continuity
> **Status:** Product meaning confirmed by the operator on 2026-10-12; exact-content approval is recorded separately in `2026-10-12-intent-continuity-approval.md`.
> **Authoritative for:** the intended experience, essential promises and product boundaries of this improvement.
> **NOT authoritative for:** architecture, current implementation status, execution authorization or the project goal; see [README](../../../README.md#why-this-exists).

## The experience, as delivered

The operator describes what they want to create and why. Together with `/intent`, they turn it into a clear picture of the finished product: who uses it, what they can do, what changes for them, and what makes the result valuable. The PRD is understandable without knowing the implementation.

While the operator and `/arch` discuss the specification, the original purpose remains visible. A technically convenient alternative cannot silently remove the reason for building the feature. The specification is checked against the agreed PRD even when its author is confident and has raised no question. A contradiction is corrected or brought back as an explicit product choice.

Once the specification is agreed, the agents prepare complete kickoffs and plans. The operator chooses what to launch and when. Within that launch's authorized scope, implementation, review, testing and repair proceed autonomously under the project's applicable rules and principles. The operator does not have to supervise routine coding decisions or repeatedly approve internal plans.

When a question needs advice, it goes directly to the responsibility that owns that kind of decision. The task advisor uses the kickoff/plan; the architecture advisor uses the specification; the intent advisor uses the PRD. Each has the relevant recorded decisions and the concrete question. There is no compulsory relay through every advisor and no requirement to keep three sessions continuously alive.

After a session is lost, an agent can recover the agreed meaning, its assignment, unresolved questions and the evidence already obtained from the documents. It knows what was checked, against which version, and what a later change requires it to check again. Earlier evidence is neither erased nor presented as proof of changed behavior.

## Reference example and rejected interpretations

The one-button installation example is an intent-preservation test case, not authorization to build that installer here. Its distinguishing promise is a prepared core of fundamental principles, rules and their tests, with the remaining stack-specific rules generated from documentation and tests generated for those rules. A library of ready-made stack templates is not an equivalent outcome merely because it is easier to deliver.

Rejected interpretations:

- Intent is only a checklist of IDs; a reader need not understand the future user experience.
- Intent matters only after implementation; losing it during specification is acceptable.
- Every advisor must receive the full conversation or remain continuously active.
- Document dependencies imply a mandatory sequence of advisor calls.
- Best implementation practices authorize changing the product promise or bypassing applicable project checks.
- A green hash/schema check proves semantic fidelity.
- The scope is only one tiny fix; the remaining agreed lifecycle is dropped.
- The scope is a replacement factory, new scheduler, new global advisor registry or a rewrite of existing skills.

## Product understanding and open questions

Permitted: choose a different internal implementation that delivers the same agreed experience and meets the applicable architecture and quality constraints. Rejected: silently substitute a different experience because the implementation is easier. A fresh advisor must explain this distinction from the documents before exercising the product responsibility.

No unresolved product preference is intentionally delegated to an agent in this brief. Technical choices about document references, checkpoint timing, recording and validation belong to `/arch`; they are not already-approved implementation details. Unrecorded preferences discovered later remain questions for the operator.

## Evaluation and boundaries

Observe fidelity to the promises, operator corrections caused by misunderstanding, successful document-only recovery, retained/current evidence and the effort spent on consultation. Record real observations and limits; no numeric savings target or universal correctness guarantee is claimed.

Do not widen this feature into implementing consumer rule generators, a new installer or a new collection of programming principles. Those existing/evolving systems provide the quality context this workflow must respect. Existing security, publication, merge and spending boundaries remain applicable.
