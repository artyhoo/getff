## Intended experience

The framework operator wants useful changes delivered sooner with less model work. Small source edits currently cause repeated maintenance of hashes, installation fingerprints and evidence. Other investigated delays include faulty check wrappers, contaminated test environments, weak acceptance tests, incomplete handoffs and unconsumed completed results. Improving only the speed of one command would leave these other causes intact.

The desired experience is an ordinary edit followed by the relevant reliable checks and an actionable result, with routine mechanical work handled by existing mechanisms. The operator should not need to reconstruct whether work is running or relay an already-authorized next action. Controls against incorrect AI-generated changes remain effective. The improvement must not become another large platform to maintain.

The requested deliverable now is a prioritized solution specification covering the investigated cause groups. Implementation, external dispatch and publication are separate transitions. A first useful increment can address the small-change maintenance chain while later slices remain explicitly open.

## Permitted deferrals

- Implementation may proceed in bounded increments; E2 applies to every increment.
- Later slices may require concrete design refinement at their existing owner boundary. They remain open, never counted as completed by the first slice.
- Fingerprint/ledger migration is conditional on residual maintenance cost; retaining the current representation is valid.
- Automatic restart of a terminated owner is a separate task. Active waiting or persisted results do not establish that outcome; unsupported unattended routes remain explicit.
- Exact account-wide token attribution, the initiator of recorded socket resets, and unexplained historical process disappearance may remain unknown. Their containment and diagnostic dispositions must be explicit.
- New external tooling selection and a universal optimization engine are outside the initial work.

## Rejected interpretations

- Faster means fewer useful controls, self-approval, automatic acceptance of changed tests, or bypassing publication permissions.
- All tests or all coordinators caused the delays.
- A checksum update proves a new behavioral review, or a historical pass certifies changed inputs.
- A written spec authorizes worker launch, paid retries, security changes, merge to main or publication.
- The operator's tentative endorsement establishes a measured optimal solution.

## Recorded decisions and open questions

The operator explicitly selected the small-edit maintenance chain first, asked to distinguish removal from automation, then requested a specification for all investigated causes ordered by expected benefit. The operator expressly questioned whether the change weakens control over AI. These requests define the scope; detailed design and numerical savings have not been approved.

The latest operator request selects this working hypothesis: stable navigation first; reliable commands and environments; complete bounded ownership plus the report producer/consumer; active completion waiting and reconnect; then a residual-cost decision on fingerprint/ledger migration. Terminated-owner recovery remains separate. The request is to update our specification with useful SPEC-A content, not to implement it or assert measured optimality.

No additional product interview is needed to write the requested specification. Concrete technical uncertainties and unmeasured benefit remain in the design, rather than being converted into operator-approved facts.
